About

The short version

I grew up in Beijing organizing people to look up — a comet named after a Chinese observatory, two total lunar eclipses on two continents. Now I study how to tell real astrophysical signals from the noise that impersonates them: transit searches in TESS photometry, eclipse depths in JWST time series, and the systematic errors that limit both.

Off the terminal I sear steaks with attention to Maillard kinetics, design roguelite games, and research markets on paper with a small household of AI agents kept under strict table manners — one house rule is enforced in code: no mind may eat data from the future. Quants call that look-ahead bias; my kitchen calls it basic etiquette. The same rule guards my eclipse photometry.
